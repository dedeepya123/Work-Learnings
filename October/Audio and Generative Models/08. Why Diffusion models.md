# 07 - Why Diffusion Models Took Over Audio Generation

## Recap

So far, we have built the following picture:

### Classical TTS

```text
Text
 ↓
Mel Spectrogram
 ↓
Vocoder
 ↓
Audio
```

### Modern Codec-Based TTS

```text
Text
 ↓
Generate Audio Representation
 ↓
Codec Decoder
 ↓
Audio
```

We learned that modern systems often generate:

```text
Audio Latents
```

or

```text
Codec Tokens
```

instead of generating waveform samples directly.

This naturally leads to the next question:

> How do we generate these audio representations?

Historically, many systems used autoregressive models.

More recently, diffusion models became extremely popular.

To understand why, we first need to understand the task itself.

---

## The Generation Problem

Suppose we want to synthesize speech from text.

Our goal is:

```text
Text
 ↓
Audio Latents
```

At first glance this may seem similar to language generation.

For example:

```text
Prompt
 ↓
LLM
 ↓
Tokens
```

Could we simply generate audio latents one at a time?

Yes.

In fact, early systems often did exactly that.

---

## The Autoregressive View

Think about how an LLM generates text.

```text
Token 1
 ↓
Token 2
 ↓
Token 3
 ↓
Token 4
```

Each prediction depends on previous predictions.

Generation proceeds sequentially.

```text
Current Output
 ↓
Predict Next Item
 ↓
Repeat
```

The same idea can be applied to audio.

```text
Audio Token 1
 ↓
Audio Token 2
 ↓
Audio Token 3
 ↓
Audio Token 4
```

---

## Why Audio Is Harder Than Text

The problem is scale.

A sentence might contain:

```text
20
50
100
tokens
```

Audio representations often contain:

```text
Hundreds
or
Thousands
```

of elements.

Speech is dense.

Every moment contains information.

Errors can accumulate across long sequences.

Generation can also become slow because every step depends on the previous step.

---

## The Key Question

Researchers began asking:

> Can we generate the entire representation differently?

Rather than predicting:

```text
One Piece
At A Time
```

what if we generated the representation all at once and progressively refined it?

This idea became the foundation of diffusion models.

---

## A Thought Experiment

Imagine you want to draw a portrait.

One strategy is:

```text
Pixel 1
 ↓
Pixel 2
 ↓
Pixel 3
 ↓
...
```

This resembles autoregressive generation.

Another strategy is:

```text
Start With Noise
 ↓
Make It Slightly Better
 ↓
Improve It Again
 ↓
Improve It Again
 ↓
Final Portrait
```

This resembles diffusion.

---

## The Core Idea of Diffusion

Diffusion is based on a surprisingly simple idea.

### Training

Take real data:

```text
Audio Latents
```

Add noise:

```text
Audio Latents
 ↓
Noise
 ↓
Corrupted Latents
```

Repeat many times until the original structure disappears.

Eventually we obtain:

```text
Pure Noise
```

---

### What Does The Model Learn?

The model learns:

```text
Given A Noisy Input

Predict
How To Remove The Noise
```

Conceptually:

```text
Noisy Data
 ↓
Model
 ↓
Cleaner Data
```

The model never directly learns:

```text
Noise → Audio
```

Instead it repeatedly learns:

```text
Noisy
 ↓
Slightly Less Noisy
```

---

## Inference

Generation starts from random noise.

```text
Noise
```

The model performs:

```text
Denoise Step 1
 ↓
Denoise Step 2
 ↓
Denoise Step 3
 ↓
...
```

until a meaningful representation appears.

Conceptually:

```text
Noise
 ↓
Audio Latents
```

---

## Why Is This Attractive?

Instead of generating elements sequentially:

```text
A
 ↓
B
 ↓
C
 ↓
D
```

the model works on the entire representation at once.

At every iteration:

```text
Current Latents
 ↓
Improve Them
```

This often improves quality and stability.

---

## Connecting It To Audio

Suppose our desired output is:

```python
[B, 100, 96]
```

representing audio features.

A diffusion model might perform:

```text
Random Noise
 ↓
Cleaner Features
 ↓
Cleaner Features
 ↓
Cleaner Features
 ↓
Final Features
```

The final features are then passed to:

```text
SoundStream Decoder
```

to reconstruct speech.

---

## What Is Actually Being Generated?

This is very important.

The diffusion model is not necessarily generating:

```text
Waveforms
```

directly.

Often it generates:

```text
Audio Latents
```

or

```text
Audio Features
```

For example:

```text
Text
 ↓
Diffusion Model
 ↓
Audio Features
 ↓
SoundStream Decoder
 ↓
Audio
```

The decoder handles waveform reconstruction.

---

## Why Diffusion Became Popular

Researchers found diffusion models often produce:

```text
Higher Quality
More Natural Outputs
Better Stability
```

for generative tasks.

This happened in:

```text
Images
Audio
Video
```

As a result, diffusion architectures rapidly became dominant.

---

## The Cost Of Diffusion

Nothing comes for free.

Instead of generating once:

```text
Input
 ↓
Output
```

we perform many refinement steps:

```text
Step 1
Step 2
Step 3
...
Step N
```

This improves quality but increases computation.

Inference becomes slower.

---

## The Fast Diffusion Trend

Researchers then asked:

> Can we keep the quality of diffusion while reducing the number of denoising steps?

Instead of:

```text
1000 Steps
```

they explored:

```text
100 Steps
50 Steps
20 Steps
8 Steps
```

You may have seen descriptions such as:

```text
8-Step DDPM Decoder
```

This simply means:

```text
Only 8 Denoising Iterations
```

which significantly improves latency.

---

## Connecting This To VoiceDM

Now many pieces begin to fit together.

A simplified VoiceDM-style pipeline looks like:

```text
Text
 ↓
VoiceDM Encoder
 ↓
Conditioning Features
 ↓
DiT
 ↓
Audio Features
 ↓
SoundStream Decoder
 ↓
Waveform Reconstruction
 ↓
Audio
```

The diffusion component is responsible for generating:

```text
Audio Features
```

not for producing waveform audio directly.

The decoder handles audio reconstruction.

---

## A Useful Mental Model

Think of diffusion as a sculptor.

A sculptor does not create the final statue in one operation.

Instead:

```text
Rough Shape
 ↓
Refine
 ↓
Refine
 ↓
Refine
 ↓
Final Statue
```

Diffusion works similarly.

```text
Noise
 ↓
Refine
 ↓
Refine
 ↓
Refine
 ↓
Audio Representation
```

Generation is an iterative refinement process.

---

## Diffusion vs Autoregressive Generation

### Autoregressive

```text
Generate Item 1
 ↓
Generate Item 2
 ↓
Generate Item 3
```

Think:

```text
LLMs
```

---

### Diffusion

```text
Noise
 ↓
Refine Entire Representation
 ↓
Refine Entire Representation
 ↓
Refine Entire Representation
```

Think:

```text
Modern Image Models
Many Modern Audio Models
```

---

## Mental Model

Keep the following picture in mind:

```text
Noise
 ↓
Diffusion Model
 ↓
Audio Latents
 ↓
Codec Decoder
 ↓
Audio
```

The diffusion model's responsibility is:

```text
Generate A Good Representation
```

The decoder's responsibility is:

```text
Convert That Representation Into Audio
```

This separation of responsibilities is a common pattern in modern audio systems.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Diffusion models generate data through iterative denoising.
- Training teaches the model how to remove noise.
- Inference starts from noise and progressively refines it.
- Diffusion models often generate audio representations rather than waveforms directly.
- Modern audio systems frequently apply diffusion in latent spaces.
- High-quality audio generation made diffusion extremely popular.
- More denoising steps usually improve quality but increase latency.
- Techniques such as 8-step diffusion reduce inference cost.

---

## Interview Takeaway

Diffusion models generate outputs by progressively refining noisy representations. Instead of predicting audio sequentially, they start from noise and repeatedly denoise until a meaningful audio representation emerges. In modern speech systems, diffusion is often used to generate codec latents or audio features, which are then converted into waveforms by a decoder such as SoundStream.
